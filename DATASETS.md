# Dataset Construction

This document describes how synthetic DARP instances are generated for benchmarking.

---

## Instance Format

Instances follow the **Cordeau DARP format**. Each file contains:

```
K  n  T  Q  L
0   x_0   y_0   s_0   tw_start_0   tw_end_0   d_0     ← depot (origin)
1   x_1   y_1   s_1   tw_start_1   tw_end_1   d_1     ← pickup 1
...
n   x_n   y_n   s_n   tw_start_n   tw_end_n   d_n     ← pickup n
n+1 x_{n+1} ...                                        ← dropoff 1
...
2n  ...                                                ← dropoff n
2n+1 ...                                               ← depot (destination)
```

**Header fields:**
- `K` — fleet size (upper bound on vehicles used)
- `n` — number of requests (pickup–dropoff pairs)
- `T` — route time horizon
- `Q` — vehicle capacity (maximum total load onboard)
- `L` — maximum ride time per passenger

**Node fields:** `id, x, y, service_time, tw_start, tw_end, demand`
- Demand is positive at pickups and negative at corresponding dropoffs
- Travel time and distance equal Euclidean distance between nodes

---

## Generation Procedure

Instances are generated by `scripts/generate_instances.jl` (small, n ≤ 100) and `scripts/generate_large_instances.jl` (large, n ≥ 150).

**Spatial layout:**
- Depot fixed at (0, 0)
- Pickup and dropoff coordinates drawn independently and uniformly from [0, `grid_size`]²
- No spatial correlation between a request's pickup and dropoff (fully random)

**Time windows:**
- All nodes share a wide time window `[0, T]`, guaranteeing feasibility by construction
- `T = ⌈4 × grid_size × √2 / 10⌉ × 10` — enough budget for approximately four full diagonal trips
- `L = T ÷ 2` — passengers may ride for at most half the total horizon

**Demands:**
- `D_max = 1` → all demands are 1 (unit variant)
- `D_max > 1` → each demand drawn uniformly from `{1, …, D_max}`

**Reproducibility:** All randomness uses `MersenneTwister(seed)`. Three seeds (42, 123, 999) are used per preset, giving three statistically independent realisations of each configuration.

---

## Demand Variants

Three variants are defined to probe different aspects of demand splitting:

| Variant | D_max | Q | Splitting behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unit` | 1 | 3 | All demands = 1; splitting never occurs |
| `multi` | 3 | 6 | Demands ∈ {1, 2, 3}; splitting possible but never forced (D_i ≤ Q always) |
| `split` | 5 | 3 | Demands ∈ {1, …, 5}; demands of 4 or 5 exceed Q=3, forcing mandatory splitting |

The `split` variant is the most operationally interesting: a single request's demand cannot fit on one vehicle, so the solver must coordinate multiple vehicles to serve it. This makes both the pricing subproblem and the integrality gap structurally harder.

---

## Preset Table

Fleet size `K` is chosen heuristically to ensure feasibility: large enough that the seed routes (one vehicle per request) can always serve all demand, but not so large as to be unrealistic.

| n | variant | K | Q | D_max | grid_size | T | L |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | multi | 2 | 3 | 3 | 10 | 60 | 30 |
| 4 | split | 3 | 3 | 5 | 10 | 60 | 30 |
| 8 | multi | 3 | 6 | 3 | 10 | 60 | 30 |
| 8 | split | 5 | 3 | 5 | 10 | 60 | 30 |
| 16 | multi | 6 | 6 | 3 | 20 | 120 | 60 |
| 16 | split | 10 | 3 | 5 | 20 | 120 | 60 |
| 32 | multi | 11 | 6 | 3 | 20 | 120 | 60 |
| 32 | split | 18 | 3 | 5 | 20 | 120 | 60 |
| 64 | multi | 22 | 6 | 3 | 30 | 170 | 85 |
| 64 | split | 35 | 3 | 5 | 30 | 170 | 85 |
| 100 | multi | 34 | 6 | 3 | 40 | 230 | 115 |
| 100 | split | 55 | 3 | 5 | 40 | 230 | 115 |

Sizing rationale (from code comments):
- `multi`: K ≈ ⌈n/3⌉ — each vehicle handles ~3 requests at average demand ~2 against capacity 6
- `split`: K ≈ ⌈n × 0.55⌉ — smaller capacity means more vehicles per request, with extra slack for split deliveries

`K` is **not enforced as a hard constraint** in the CG/IP formulations. The master problem is a pure covering problem with no fleet limit; `K` only controls the number of slots in the solution output.

## File Naming

```
n<N>_k<K>_q<Q>_<variant>_<seed>.txt
```

Examples:
- `n16_k10_q3_split_42.txt` — 16 requests, K=10, Q=3, split variant, seed 42
- `n100_k34_q6_multi_999.txt` — 100 requests, K=34, Q=6, multi variant, seed 999

---

## Generating Instances

```bash
# Small instances (n = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 100) — all variants, 3 seeds each
julia --project=. scripts/generate_job_list.jl \
    experiments/cg_vs_ip_small/instances \
    experiments/cg_vs_ip_small/jobs.txt

# Large CG-only run (n = 32, 64, 100 — multi + split only)
DARP_VARIANTS="multi,split" DARP_SIZES="32,64,100" \
julia --project=. scripts/generate_job_list.jl \
    experiments/cg_large/instances \
    experiments/cg_large/jobs.txt
```

Environment variable filters accepted by `generate_job_list.jl`:

| Variable | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `DARP_VARIANTS` | `"multi,split"` | Comma-separated variants to include |
| `DARP_SIZES` | `"4,8,16,32,64,100"` | Comma-separated n values (or `"all"`) |
| `DARP_IP_MAX_N` | `20` | Max n for IP solvers in the job list |
